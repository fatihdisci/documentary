# Carolina parakeet research summary

Research checked: 26 July 2026

## Editorially safe facts

- The accepted scientific name is *Conuropsis carolinensis*. It is listed as extinct by the American Ornithological Society.
- It was the only parrot native to the eastern and central United States. Its range ran through parts of the east, south and middle of the country, but recent work indicates that the range was smaller and more divided than older maps suggested.
- The bird was a medium-sized, long-tailed parrot. It had a green body, a yellow head and neck, and orange-red on the face and wing area.
- Historical accounts place it in river woods, swamps, open woodland, forest edges and farm land. It used holes in trees and was often seen in noisy groups.
- Its diet included seeds, fruit, nuts and crops. Accounts also link it with cockleburs, but the historical record is incomplete; the narration does not claim that this food made the bird poisonous.
- Clearing mature woods and wetlands, shooting by farmers and hunters, and collecting birds or feathers all put pressure on the species. The exact role of disease, nesting-site competition and other factors is unresolved.
- The last known captive bird, a male called Incas, died at Cincinnati Zoo on 21 February 1918. Later wild reports have been debated and do not prove a surviving population.
- A 2021 study treated eastern and western populations separately and estimated that they vanished decades apart. This is useful context, but it does not make any late sighting certain.

## Critical uncertainties

- The exact last wild bird and date are not known. The film says that confirmed wild groups had faded by the early twentieth century rather than naming a final wild sighting.
- The disappearance cannot be reduced to one proven cause. Human pressure is clear; the final collapse remains a historical puzzle.
- Many details of nesting, movement and daily life come from scattered historical reports. The film uses careful language such as "accounts suggest" where needed.

## Editorial decision

The episode treats this as a bright, familiar bird lost through several human pressures, not as a mystery with a simple answer. The hard ending is documented; the last days in the wild are not.
