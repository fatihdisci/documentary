# Chinese paddlefish — Research and sources

Research date: 2026-07-28

## Research summary

- **Accepted name used in this package:** Chinese paddlefish, *Psephurus gladius*. The Latin name appears only in metadata and research notes, never in the spoken narration.
- **Range:** The species was native to China's longest river system and the water near its mouth. Its life depended on a connected route between lower feeding areas and upper breeding areas.
- **Appearance:** A very large silver-gray fish with a pale underside, a long flat paddle-shaped snout, small eyes, a broad mouth, and a forked tail.
- **Size:** Reliable reference records support a length of about three metres and a weight of up to about three hundred kilograms. Older reports of seven metres are disputed, so the narration says only that the fish could reach several metres.
- **Food:** Unlike the living American paddlefish, the Chinese paddlefish hunted other fish.
- **River journey:** Adults moved upstream to lay eggs. Young fish later moved toward the middle and lower river. A clear route through the river was essential.
- **Fishing pressure:** The fish was once common enough for the yearly catch to reach about twenty-five tons in the nineteen seventies. Direct fishing, accidental catches, and the loss of large adults drove a steep decline.
- **The dam:** A major dam closed across the river in 1981. It had no route that allowed the paddlefish to pass, so adults below it could not reach important breeding water upstream.
- **Last confirmed sighting:** A living paddlefish caught by accident in early 2003 was treated, tagged, and released. The tracking signal was lost within hours. No later sighting has been confirmed.
- **Searches:** Surveys from 2006 to 2008 found no confirmed fish. A wider river search in 2017 and 2018 recorded 332 other kinds of fish but no Chinese paddlefish.
- **Extinction window:** A peer-reviewed study using 210 records placed the loss by 2005, and no later than 2010.
- **Current status:** IUCN confirmed the species as Extinct in its 2022 update.
- **Main lesson:** Several pressures worked together. Heavy fishing reduced the population, the dam blocked a vital journey, and rescue efforts began too late to build a safe group outside the river.

## Accuracy notes

- Do not call the Chinese paddlefish a seven-metre fish as a settled fact.
- Do not blame one dam alone. Fishing had already caused a major decline.
- Do not say the fish was officially declared extinct in 2020. The study appeared in 2020; IUCN confirmed the Extinct status in 2022.
- Do not claim a precise death date for the last animal. The strongest estimate places the species' final loss between 2005 and 2010.
- Do not say the last tagged fish died when its signal was lost. Its later fate is unknown.
- Do not use the Latin name or difficult place names in TTS narration.
